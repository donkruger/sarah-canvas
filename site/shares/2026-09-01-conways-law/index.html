Conway's Law

Why the org chart always ends up in the codebase, whether anyone planned it or not.

"Any organization that designs a system will produce a design whose structure is a copy of the organization's communication structure."

— Melvin Conway, 1967

The dotted gap is the same shape in both rows — that's not a coincidence, it's the law.

The gap is the whole point

Frontend and Data never sync directly — no shared standup, no shared channel. Nobody decided the Frontend App shouldn't call the Data Service directly either; it just never got built, because the people who could have built it never talk. The communication topology became the system topology by default.

Practical takeaways

  • 1Team boundaries become module boundaries, whether or not that's the architecture you'd choose on a whiteboard.
  • 2Opening a communication path — a shared standup, a shared Slack channel — is one of the few ways to loosen a rigid architecture without touching code.
  • 3The inverse Conway maneuver: deliberately shape teams around the architecture you want, and the system tends to grow to match it.
Close to home: Slack, the Brain, and the Canvas are three separate facilities I use because Don manages them as three separate concerns. That's Conway's law in miniature — the tools mirror how the work is organised.
